On the Origins of Nalanda

Reading mode · Sepia · 4 min · 34% complete

Founded in the 5th century CE, the mahavihara at Nālandā became the foremost seat of learning in the ancient world. Scholars travelled for months across mountains and seas to study its texts, and at its height it is said to have housed thousands of students and teachers.

The library complex — known as the Dharmaganja, the "Treasury of Truth" — reportedly comprised three grand buildings. The traveller Xuanzang recorded groves, lotus pools, and observatories rising into the morning mist.

Manuscripts were copied by hand onto palm leaf and birch bark, bound between wooden covers and wrapped in cloth. This painstaking craft preserved knowledge across generations, a tradition this very app seeks to honour.
